# Manitoba Soil Data & Intelligence Hub

### Phase 1 — Soil Data Integration, Discovery, and AI-Assisted Intelligence

*Built for Manitoba. Connected to Canada. Interoperable globally.*

**[Explore the Interactive Demonstration](https://digitalagroecosystemslab.github.io/manitoba-data-hub-demo/)**

> **Ask the data, not the database.**

## 1. Project Overview

The Manitoba Soil Data & Intelligence Hub is a proposed research initiative led by the [Digital AgroEcosystems Lab](https://www.digitalagroecosystemslab.ca/) at the University of Manitoba.

The initiative aims to develop an interoperable and intelligent framework for discovering, connecting, visualizing, and interpreting Manitoba's soil information.

Manitoba has substantial soil information collected through government programs, academic research, soil laboratories, and environmental monitoring. However, these datasets may differ in formats, geographic coverage, accessibility, analytical methods, and documentation.

The proposed Hub will investigate approaches to connecting these resources while respecting data ownership, privacy, and existing institutional infrastructure.

Rather than creating another isolated database, the project envisions a connected data and intelligence layer that makes existing soil information easier to discover, understand, and use.

## 2. Phase 1: Soil Information

The initial research and development phase focuses specifically on soil data and soil-related intelligence in Manitoba.

Key objectives include:

1. **Soil Data Discovery:** Develop methods for identifying available soil datasets, their metadata, spatial coverage, and accessibility.
2. **FAIR Data Interoperability:** Investigate approaches for improving the Findability, Accessibility, Interoperability, and Reusability of soil information across different data providers.
3. **Geospatial Soil Intelligence:** Explore interactive maps, visualizations, and analytical tools for understanding soil properties and their spatial variability.
4. **AI-Assisted Data Interaction:** Investigate natural-language interfaces that allow users to ask soil-related questions and receive interpretable, evidence-based responses.
5. **Transparency and Uncertainty:** Develop methods for communicating data sources, analytical procedures, quality limitations, and uncertainty.

The longer-term vision may include additional agricultural and environmental information, such as crops, climate, water, and grasslands. These domains are outside the initial Phase 1 scope.

## 3. Interactive Demonstration

This repository hosts a publicly accessible demonstration of the proposed platform's user interface.

The demonstration illustrates how users could interact with integrated soil information through a single digital environment.

### Demonstration Features

* **Ask:** Explore five predefined soil-related questions and their example responses.
* **Explore Data:** Examine the presentation and organization of example datasets.
* **Maps:** Explore illustrative spatial information and geographic boundaries.
* **Dashboards:** View soil-related indicators and visualizations.
* **Data Catalogue:** Explore how soil data resources and metadata could be documented.
* **FAIR & Metadata:** Examine concepts supporting data interoperability and reuse.
* **Answer Transparency:** Explore how supporting evidence, analytical methods, limitations, and uncertainty could be communicated.

### Demonstration Limitations

> **Important:** This publicly hosted demonstration uses entirely fabricated data, identifiers, geographic boundaries, connections, and results.

It is not an operational government platform and does not provide authoritative soil information.

The hosted demonstration uses predefined responses rather than a live AI model.

A separate local prototype also explores language-model-assisted interaction, where an AI model explains results generated through analytical procedures rather than serving as the underlying scientific data source.

## 4. Research and Development Direction

The proposed research will explore several interconnected components:

* **Data integration:** Connecting distributed soil datasets through documented metadata, interoperable structures, and appropriate data-access mechanisms.
* **Soil analytics:** Supporting reproducible statistical, geospatial, and machine-learning workflows to extract meaningful information.
* **AI-assisted interaction:** Developing approaches for translating natural-language questions into structured data queries and interpretable outputs.
* **Responsible data management:** Considering data provenance, uncertainty, privacy, access permissions, and institutional ownership throughout the system design.

These components represent research directions and proposed capabilities, not a claim that all functions have already been implemented.

## 5. Research Leadership and Collaboration

### Digital AgroEcosystems Lab — University of Manitoba

The initiative is being developed by [Dr. Nasem Badreldin](https://umanitoba.ca/agricultural-food-sciences/soil-science/nasem-badreldin), Assistant Professor of Digital Agronomy and Principal Investigator of the [Digital AgroEcosystems Lab](https://www.digitalagroecosystemslab.ca/) in the Department of Soil Science, University of Manitoba.

The Lab works in digital agriculture, soil spectroscopy, remote sensing, geospatial analytics, and artificial intelligence.

Existing soil spectroscopy and research data activities at the University of Manitoba offer opportunities to explore and validate elements of the proposed framework.

### University of Manitoba

The University of Manitoba provides the academic and research environment supporting the development of this concept.

Existing research infrastructure, soil laboratory capabilities, and geospatial expertise could contribute to future development and evaluation.

### Government of Manitoba

The Government of Manitoba is an important prospective collaborator in defining provincial soil information needs, exploring data interoperability, and identifying practical applications.

Future engagement would help align research priorities with provincial requirements and existing data-management practices.

> **Partnership and funding disclaimer:** This demonstration does not imply a formal partnership, funding commitment, government authorization, or institutional endorsement. Institutional logos are included for identification of proposed collaborators only.

## 6. Future Vision

Following development and evaluation of the soil-focused framework, the architecture could potentially support other agricultural and environmental domains.

The long-term ambition is to investigate a scalable, interoperable framework supporting evidence-based research, agricultural innovation, and decision-making across Manitoba and beyond.

Expansion to additional domains would require separate research priorities, appropriate collaborations, and further development.
